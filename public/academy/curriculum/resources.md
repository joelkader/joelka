# Resources — Data, Reading & Tools Field Guide

Everything here is chosen for one purpose: building the "uses all resources to determine and locate relevant
data" and "continues to build industry knowledge" muscles — mostly free.

## Free data (bookmark all of these)

| Source | What you pull from it |
|---|---|
| **EIA** (eia.gov + open API) | Form **860** (every US generator), **923** (monthly generation/fuel), **861** (utility sales/customers), **STEO/AEO** (forecasts), Henry Hub & power prices |
| **FERC** | **Form 1** (utility plant costs & O&M detail — rate-case raw material), eLibrary dockets (rate cases, interconnection disputes, co-location fights) |
| **ISO/RTO portals** | PJM DataMiner2, ERCOT MIS, CAISO OASIS, MISO/SPP/NYISO/ISO-NE — LMPs, load, capacity auction results, **interconnection queues** |
| **gridstatus.io** | Clean aggregated ISO data (prices, fuel mix, load) when raw portals fight you |
| **EPA CAMD/CEMS** | Hourly unit-level emissions → implied generation for any fossil plant (competitor tracking, free) |
| **NRC** | Daily power reactor status report — the nuclear fleet every morning |
| **EDGAR + full-text search** (efts.sec.gov) | Filings; FTS is your free AlphaSense — search "co-location", "take-or-pay", "capacity contract" across all filings |
| **LBNL** | Interconnection queue studies ("Queued Up"), utility-scale cost/market reports |
| **NREL / Lazard** | Technology cost benchmarks, the famous LCOE+ annual survey |
| **Company IR sites** | Decks (hedge tables! backlog slides!), transcripts, 8-Ks — the spine of your fact packs |
| **TIKR / Koyfin free tiers** | Consensus estimates & comps practice without a terminal |
| **FRED** | Rates (10-yr, SOFR) for WACC/cap-rate context |

**Weekly 20-minute ritual:** one ISO datapoint (what did power/gas do and why), one filing skim from a company
you're tracking, one flashcard-worthy fact added to your notes. Industry knowledge compounds like interest.

## Terminals (know the names before day one)

- **Bloomberg:** DES, FA, EE/EEO/MODL (estimates), ANR, EQRV/RV, HDS, DDIS (debt ladder), SPLC, BI, GP/GF,
  NI POW/UTI/NUK, XLTP/BQL (Excel). Module 7 Lesson 3 has the full table; the Data Hunt drill tests it.
- **FactSet:** screening, estimates, ownership, `=FDS` Excel codes — most banks' comp sheets run on it.
- **Others you'll hear:** Visible Alpha (line-item consensus), AlphaSense (document search), Tegus (expert
  calls), S&P Capital IQ / Platts (curves), BNEF (energy transition data), Wood Mackenzie, Yes Energy /
  Enverus (power data), SemiAnalysis (compute/datacenter research).

## Books (in order of payoff)

1. **Rosenbaum & Pearl, *Investment Banking*** — the standard for valuation/comps/LBO mechanics. Do the exercises.
2. **McKinsey, *Valuation*** — deeper theory; read after R&P.
3. **Davis Edwards, *Energy Trading & Investing*** — the best single bridge into power/gas markets math.
4. **Meredith Angwin, *Shorting the Grid*** — RTO/capacity-market politics, readable and opinionated (argue with it).
5. **Gretchen Bakke, *The Grid*** — narrative history; good mental furniture.
6. **Pratt's/Yescombe, *Principles of Project Finance*** — when you're serious about the infra-fund path.

## Podcasts & newsletters (commute curriculum)

- **Catalyst (Shayle Kann)** and **The Interchange** — energy tech/finance, guest quality is high.
- **Odd Lots** — the AI-power-datacenter episodes are near-perfect interview prep.
- **Volts (David Roberts)** — policy/grid depth.
- **Utility Dive / RTO Insider** — the trade press analysts actually read (RTO Insider for market governance).
- **SemiAnalysis** — datacenter/GPU supply chain; the neocloud analyst's homework.
- **Grid Status blog / LBNL releases** — data-driven grid updates.

## Practice targets (real-world reps)

When you're ready to leave the fictional sandbox, these public-company categories map to the labs
(study their IR decks and try to rebuild one schedule from each):
merchant IPPs and nuclear operators (hedge ladders, capacity tables) → Labs 1–3;
regulated utilities (rate base CAGR slides, capex plans) → Lab 7;
data center REITs (leasing stats, development pipelines, $/MW) → Lab 4;
listed GPU clouds (backlog/RPO, depreciation policy, debt terms) → Lab 5;
yieldcos (CAFD bridges, PPA profiles) → Lab 6.

## Courses (optional; the academy + books cover the substance)

Wall Street Prep / BIWS if you want guided Excel repetition and a certificate line for the résumé;
Macabacus (free) for modeling conventions and templates. CFA L1 is respected but slow — only start it if
targeting buy-side research specifically and you have the runway.
